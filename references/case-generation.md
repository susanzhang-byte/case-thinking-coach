# Case generation protocol

## Inputs and difficulty

Use optional role, category, industry, difficulty, duration, quantitative intensity, interaction mode, and provenance. Defaults: intermediate, consumer marketing or strategy, moderate math, fully fictional.

- **Introductory:** clear objective, few branches, light math, guided synthesis.
- **Intermediate:** ambiguity, prioritization, multiple evidence points, moderate math.
- **Advanced:** incomplete information, conflicting signals, tradeoffs, distractors, executive synthesis.

## Internal build sequence

1. Choose a learning objective and fingerprint: `category × industry × decision-maker × objective × constraint × customer × channel × data pattern`.
2. Apply the theme gate in [safety.md](safety.md).
3. Draft the objective, metric, prompt, interviewer facts, and exhibits.
4. Create a tailored reference structure and a credible alternative.
5. Solve calculations with aligned units and periods.
6. Confirm evidence supports a defensible decision and identify risks.
7. Screen the complete case again.
8. Present only candidate-facing information until the interaction calls for more.

If the conversation contains a similar fingerprint, vary at least three meaningful dimensions.

## Provenance

Use `CURATED`, `CURRENT`, `USER-SOURCED`, and `FICTIONAL`. For current-inspired work, distinguish `RESEARCHED CONTEXT`, `FICTIONALIZED CONTEXT`, and `SIMULATED DATA`; create a substantially new case and reveal inspiration sources only after the exercise. Never present simulated figures as real-company facts.

Preferred sources: official company/government/regulatory materials; public McKinsey, BCG, and Bain interview-preparation materials for evaluation principles; reputable business/news publications; strong trade or research institutions. Avoid anonymous posts, aggregators, unverifiable claims, and leaked cases.

Every maintained case includes its learning objective, role, category, industry, difficulty, duration, provenance, prompt, metric, interviewer facts, exhibits, reference framework, alternative, calculations, recommendation, rubric notes, assumptions, and safety review.
