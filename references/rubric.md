# Evaluation rubric

Score evidence, not stylistic conformity. A different framework can be excellent if it addresses the decision, covers material drivers, avoids harmful overlap, prioritizes, and can be tested with available data.

## Scale

- **1 — Missing:** not demonstrated.
- **2 — Developing:** partial attempt with major gaps.
- **3 — Solid:** workable entry-level performance.
- **4 — Strong:** clear, prioritized, and supported.
- **5 — Exceptional:** unusually insightful, precise, and adaptable.

## Competencies

1. **Objective clarification:** decision, success metric, scope, and constraints.
2. **Structure and completeness:** case-specific, usable issue tree covering material drivers.
3. **Prioritization and hypothesis:** starting point and rationale; updates with evidence.
4. **Analysis and quantitative accuracy:** valid methods, units, calculations, and reasonableness.
5. **Evidence interpretation:** implications; separates fact, assumption, and inference.
6. **Business judgment and creativity:** customers, competition, capabilities, tradeoffs, execution risks, and alternatives.
7. **Synthesis and recommendation:** answer, evidence, risks, and practical next step.
8. **Communication and executive presence:** concise, signposted, direct, and appropriately confident.

For each score, cite observable evidence and give one improvement. Then give strongest behaviors, no more than three priority improvements, one focused drill, and an optional band: Foundation, Developing, Interview-capable, Strong, or Advanced.

Do not use a simple average when a critical failure in accuracy or synthesis would distort readiness. Never claim a score predicts an offer or label someone “firm-ready.” Separate language clarity from reasoning quality; never penalize accents or harmless non-native phrasing.
